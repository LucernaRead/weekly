THE BIG STORY · 头条
America Votes : Election Day Comes on Tuesday, November 3
By The Lucerna newsroom · Reading level : A2
Tuesday, November 3, is Election
Day in the United States. It is a
midterm election — an election in
the middle of a president's
four-year term. All 435 seats in the
House of Representatives are on
the ballot, along with about a third
of the Senate and governorships in
many states.
In many states, voting has already
started. Early voting lets people
vote before Election Day : you go to
a voting place, you show your ID,
you mark your ballot, and your
vote counts on election night. On
Tuesday, the voting places open
early in the morning and close in
the evening.
What happens on Tuesday night ?
Television channels show maps
that slowly change colour, state by
state. But nobody should expect
every result at midnight : each state
runs its own election, and counting
takes time. Some races are called
on Tuesday night ; others take days.
And that is only half of the week.
On Friday, November 6, two big
films open in cinemas. On Sunday,
November 8, American football
goes to Madrid, Spain, and
Formula 1 races in São Paulo,
Brazil. Our sport dossier is on page
4.
This is the second issue of The
Lucerna in English. Last week, you
promised us letters — and this
week, our Readers' Corner on page
7 tells you honestly where those
letters stand. Welcome back !

的结果。

One More Hour of
Sleep : America Falls
Back
By The Lucerna newsroom · Reading level : A2
On Sunday, November 1, at 2 a.m.,
the clocks in most of the United
States went back one hour — to 1
a.m. again. The hour from 1 to 2
happened twice. Americans call it «
falling back », and the prize is
simple : one more hour of sleep.
Why do we change the clocks ? In
summer, we move the clocks
forward so the evenings stay light
longer. In autumn, we move them
back, so the mornings are light
again for school and work. The
American memory phrase is :
spring forward, fall back.
Not every place changes. Arizona
and Hawaii stay on the same time
all year — no spring forward, no
fall back. If you call a friend in
Phoenix on Sunday, remember :
their clock did not move.
The price of the extra hour is a
darker evening. From this week,
the sun sets earlier, and the walk
home from school or work is in the
dark. Lights go on in windows, and
autumn evenings begin.
And what about Saturday night ?
On Saturday, October 31, it was
Halloween. Children in costumes
went from house to house with the
old call : « Trick or treat ! » In the
morning, the pumpkins were still
on the porches — and the candy
bags were full.
Halloween and the clock change
belong together in America : one
night of costumes, then one night
of an extra hour. Two autumn
rituals, one weekend apart — and
both are now just passed, not
coming.
DO THE MATHS · 算一算
From 2 a.m. back to 1 a.m. : the night
of November 1 has 25 hours, not 24.
One extra hour — one extra hour of
sleep, if you use it well.

Friday Night at the
Movies : A Cat and a
Monster
By The Lucerna newsroom · Reading level : A2
→ B1
On Friday, November 6, two very
different films open in cinemas
across America. One is for children
and families. The other is for
monster fans. Both give you a
reason to go out on a dark autumn
evening.
The first is « The Cat in the Hat »,
from the famous children's book by
Dr. Seuss. The Cat — tall, funny,
and always in trouble — comes to
life on the big screen, with Bill
Hader as the voice of the Cat. If you
know the book, you know the hat :
red and white, and much too tall.
The second is « Godzilla Minus
Zero », the new film from Japanese
director Takashi Yamazaki.
Godzilla is the giant monster who
has walked through Japanese films
for more than seventy years. This
time, the monster returns to the
cinema — bigger, darker, and
louder than ever.
Which film would you choose ? A
talking cat in a striped hat, or a
giant monster in a city ? In our
newsroom, the vote is split — half
for the cat, half for the monster.
Tell us your choice on page 7.
AT HOME THIS WEEK · 在家看什么
Wed Nov 4 — « A Dog's Perfect
Christmas » and « Taken : Natalee
Holloway » arrive on Netflix.
Thu Nov 5 — « Black Doves » Season 2
begins. Fri Nov 6 — « Under Siege »
Season 1 and « Unprotected : Murder

Sun Nov 8 — « Love Is Blind : The

A film is not only a story — it is also
English practice. Listen for three
things : the catchphrase (the line
everybody repeats), the joke (why
does the room laugh ?), and the
ending (happy, sad, or open ?).
After the film, try to tell the story
in five English sentences.
And if you stay at home, the same
rule works for a series : one
episode, five sentences, in English.
The best five-sentence reviews will
be printed in VOL.3 — send yours
to the newsroom.

FIVE SENTENCES
After any film or episode, tell the story
in five English sentences : who, where,
what happened, the surprise, the

Sunday in Madrid, São
Paulo and Miami
By The Lucerna newsroom · Reading level : B1
Sunday, November 8, is the biggest
sports day of the autumn. Three
countries, five sports, one Sunday
— and American football in a
Spanish football stadium.
In Madrid, the NFL plays its
international game at the Santiago
Bernabéu : the Cincinnati Bengals
against the Atlanta Falcons.
American football in the home of
Real Madrid — a strange and
wonderful mix. The game is part of
NFL Week 9, and it is the week's
most talked-about fixture.
Back in the United States, Week 9
continues the same day : the Miami
Dolphins play the Detroit Lions at
Hard Rock Stadium in Miami
Gardens. Two games, two
continents, one league — that is the
modern NFL.
In Brazil, Formula 1 races the São
Paulo Grand Prix, the second race
after the United States Grand Prix
in Austin. Fast cars, a famous
track, and a championship still to
decide — the perfect Sunday
afternoon for racing fans.
And there is more : NASCAR races
at Homestead-Miami, the WTA
Finals begin at Indian Wells, and in
Arlington, Texas, Alycia
Baumgardner defends her women's
lightweight title against Elif Nur
Turhan. One Sunday is not enough
— good thing the week has seven
days.
WHY MADRID ? · 为什么去马德里？
The NFL plays games outside the
United States every year, so new fans
can see the sport live. Madrid joins
London, Munich and São Paulo on
the international map.
How do you follow five sports in
one day ? Choose one game to
watch fully, and follow the others
on your phone. That is the modern
fan : one screen for the game, one
screen for the scores.
On Saturday, November 7, the
warm-up is already on : Major
League Soccer plays its late-season
fixtures, including Charlotte
against Inter Miami and Atlanta
against the New York Red Bulls.
The weekend starts on Saturday —
and it does not stop.

Election Night at
Home : Maps, Phones
and Pizza
By The Lucerna newsroom · Reading level : B1
Last week, we asked you a question
: what do Americans read, watch
and eat while they wait for the
results ? On Tuesday night,
November 3, here is the answer —
from living rooms across the
country.
First, they watch the maps. On
television, the map of the United
States slowly changes colour, state
by state, as results come in. Every
channel has its own map, its own
experts, and its own way of saying
the same sentence : « Too close to
call. » That means : we do not
know yet.
Second, they eat. Election night is
pizza night in many homes — easy
food, no cooking, hands free for
the phone. Popcorn, chips and
soda join the pizza on the table.
Nobody cooks a big dinner on a
night when the news never stops.
Third, they read — on their phones.
Friends send messages : « Did you
see Ohio ? » « Florida is close ! » «
Go to bed, we will know tomorrow.
» The second screen is part of the
night : the television for the map,
the phone for the friends.
And the children ? They watch too,
from the sofa, and they ask the best
questions : « Why is that state red ?
» « Why does it take so long ? » «
Can we stay up until the end ? »
The answer to the last question is
usually : no. The results will still be
there in the morning.
TOO CLOSE TO CALL · 难分胜负
When a race is very close, channels
do not call a winner. They wait. « Too
close to call » is the most honest
sentence on election night — and the
most repeated.
If you want to practise
election-night English, try this :
watch ten minutes of results
coverage in English, and write
down five sentences you hear. « Too
close to call » will certainly be one
of them. The other four are your
homework.
译注｜讲解｜a map （选举）地图 · too
close to call 难分胜负、无法判定 · a
second screen 第二块屏幕（手机）。

How America Votes :
One Country, Fifty
Elections
By The Lucerna newsroom · Reading level : B2
There is no single American
election. There are fifty — one in
every state — run by the states
themselves, under rules that differ
from state to state. That is why
Election Day looks different in
Texas, in Florida and in Ohio, and
why the results arrive at different
speeds.
The House of Representatives has
435 members, each serving two
years. Every midterm, all 435 seats
are on the ballot. The Senate has
100 members, each serving six
years, and only about a third of
them face the voters in any one
election. Governors, who lead the
states, are also on the ballot in
many states.
Why does a midterm matter ?
Because the party that holds the
House and the Senate can shape
the laws of the next two years. A
midterm can change who controls
Congress — or confirm the voters'
choice of two years before. Nothing
is decided in advance ; that is the
point of the vote.
How does a voter vote ? First,
register — in most states, before
Election Day. Then, choose : vote
early in person, vote by mail where
the state allows it, or vote in person
on Tuesday. At the voting place,
show your ID where the state asks
for it, mark your ballot in private,
and put it in the box. The sticker —
« I VOTED » — is optional, but
almost everybody takes one.
And after Tuesday ? Counting
continues. Mail ballots take time to
open and check. Close races take
longer. Channels call a race only
when the maths is clear — and
when it is not clear, they say the
honest sentence : too close to call.
Patience is part of the system, not a
failure of it.

A note on numbers in this issue :
this paper prints no poll figures
and no predictions. Polls change,
and a learner's paper should teach

LEVEL A2 · 基础
FALLING BACK
« The clocks fell back. » Fall back = to
go back one hour. The past is fell back,
not « falled back ». The repeated hour
on November 1 is the example of the
week (page 2).
GOING TO + VERB
For a plan : « She is going to vote on
Tuesday. » be + going to + the verb,
with no change. It is the friendliest
future in English — use it for every plan
of the week.
THE EXAMPLE · 例句
« Americans are going to vote on
Tuesday. » — the election story, from
page 1.
LEVEL B1 · 进阶
COMPARATIVES
Short adjective + -er + than : « Madrid
is bigger than most stadiums. » Long
adjective : more + adjective : « more
convenient ». And good becomes better
— never « more good ». Page 4 is full of
them.
THE PAST SIMPLE
Regular verbs add -ed : changed,
watched. Irregular verbs change : fall
→ fell, go → went, choose → chose.
Sports and election pages are written
in this tense — every result is past.
THE EXAMPLE · 例句
« The clocks fell back on Sunday,
November 1. » — fall → fell (page 2).
LEVEL B2 · 挑战
HEADLINE PRESENT
Headlines speak of yesterday in the
present : « America votes », « Bengals
play in Madrid ». The present simple
makes old news feel live. Read the front
page again and find three.
WHETHER = IF ?
« Nobody knows whether the race is
called yet. » Whether introduces an
open question inside a sentence —
more elegant than if, and safe
everywhere. It is the word of the deep
read (page 6).
THE EXAMPLE · 例句
« Patience is part of the system, not a
failure of it. » — part of = 一部分 (page
6).
