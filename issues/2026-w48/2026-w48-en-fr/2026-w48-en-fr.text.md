Black Friday Week: The Sales Started Early

By The Lucerna newsroom　Reading level: A2

On Friday, November 27, the shops open early for Black Friday — the biggest shopping day of the year. But the sales did not wait for Friday. In Britain, Currys started its sale on October 28, and Argos opened its sale at the end of October. Amazon's Black Friday Week runs from Thursday, November 19, to Cyber Monday, Monday, November 30 — twelve days of deals.

Black Friday is one day. The sales last all month. The shops call it “Black November”: a month of offers, not a day. And the warnings are out too. Which?, the consumer group, tracked 175 products through the 2024 sales — and found none of them at its cheapest price of the year on Black Friday itself. For Amazon, 88% of the deals it tracked in 2024 were the same price, or cheaper, at another time of the year.

So the rule for this week is simple: check the price before you buy. A bargain is not a bargain if you did not need it. A price cut is only a cut if the old price was real. This issue shows you how to read a deal — and how to say “no” to it, in English.

There is help with winter, too. The Budget of October 28 gives £100 to many homes in England this winter, to help pay for energy — for homes in Council Tax bands A to D. The 5p cut in fuel duty, and the VAT cut on home electricity, run until July 2027.

Welcome to VOL.5 — and to the Christmas countdown.

LEVEL A2　Advent begins　L'Avent commence

The First Sunday of Advent: Four Candles, Four Weeks

By The Lucerna newsroom　Reading level: A2

On Sunday, November 29, the first candle is lit. It is the first Sunday of Advent — four Sundays of waiting, four candles, and then Christmas Day, on Friday, December 25. In Britain, Advent is the quiet start of Christmas: the markets open, the lights go on, and the countdown begins.

The Christmas tree has a long story in Britain. The first royal tree stood at Windsor in December 1800, put up by Queen Charlotte, the German-born wife of King George III. But the tree became famous in 1848, when the Illustrated London News printed a picture of Queen Victoria, Prince Albert and their children around a candlelit tree at Windsor Castle. The tree in the picture stood on a table — not on the floor. In 1850, an American magazine printed the same picture, with the royal family changed into an ordinary family.

The tree stood on a table, not on the floor. That is the sentence to remember from this page — and the picture on this page is the American copy, from Godey's Lady's Book, December 1850.

This year, the Christmas countdown has a new face. On Tuesday, November 3, John Lewis showed its Christmas advert for 2026: “Gloria and Pickles”. Gloria, played by Dame Kristin Scott Thomas, is a grandmother who wants to give her grandchildren a real present — not money in a card. Pickles, her old toy dog, comes to life and talks her into one last shopping trip. The song is Sam Fender's cover of the Rolling Stones' “You Can't Always Get What You Want”. The line is: “Don't give up on giving.” It is the twentieth John Lewis Christmas advert.

And on Monday, November 30, Scotland has its own day: St Andrew's Day, a bank holiday in Scotland. St Andrew is Scotland's patron saint — and the day is the last day of the sales, Cyber Monday, too. One day, two reasons to stay at home — or to go out.

Four candles, four weeks. Light one each Sunday, and Christmas will come.

In the jungle, a star is a meal. That is the sentence to remember — and the reason the trials matter. A star is not a prize for the wall. It is dinner.

Why does Britain watch? Because the jungle is honest television: famous people, no make-up, no phones, and rice and beans for dinner. And because the launch is a national moment: the show usually starts on a Sunday, but this year it starts on a Monday — because England played Spain in the Nations League on Sunday, November 15.

LEVEL B2　The Budget　Le budget

The Budget and Your Winter Bills

By The Lucerna newsroom　Reading level: B2

On Wednesday, October 28, the Chancellor of the Exchequer, John Healey, stood up in the House of Commons and read the Budget. It was his first Budget — and the first Budget of the new Prime Minister, Andy Burnham. The slogan was “Breathing Space now, Hope for the Future”. In plain English: help now, hope later.

What is a budget? It is the government's plan for the year: how much money it will take in tax, and how much it will spend. A budget is a plan, not a present. The money has to come from somewhere — and this Budget says it will not come from higher taxes on working people. That promise was in the government's 2024 manifesto, and the Chancellor says he will keep it.

So what changes for you? Three things this winter. First, £100 for energy: households in Council Tax bands A to D in England get a £100 Household Energy Payment this winter. Second, the 5p cut in fuel duty — the tax on petrol and diesel — runs until July 2027. Third, the VAT cut on electricity at home runs until July 2027 too. A hundred pounds does not buy a winter — but it buys a warmer one.

What else? The Budget also raises money in other places: a temporary 3% rise in the tax on the profits of banks and building societies, for fifteen months from January 2027. And the Finance Bill — the law that turns the Budget into rules — was introduced in Parliament on November 3.

What happens next? The Budget is not one day. The Welsh Government publishes its draft budget on November 17. The Scottish Parliament sets its tax rates on December 3. Parliament debates the Finance Bill, line by line. And the help arrives in the winter — when the bills arrive too.

A note on numbers in this issue: this paper prints the Budget of October 28, and says so. The numbers are the ones the Chancellor read to the House of Commons. When the Finance Bill becomes law, VOL.6 will report what changed.

A PRACTICE BILL　Une facture

Your winter bill: electricity £120 a month · gas £90 a month. The £100 payment covers most of one month of electricity. The numbers are practice numbers — they teach how to read a bill, not what you will pay.

This issue went to press on Monday, November 16, 2026. The Welsh draft budget comes out on Tuesday, November 17 — one day after press time. VOL.6 will carry the Welsh numbers.

WORDS　A2　bargain

Explication | bargain veut dire « bonne affaire » : le prix est bon, l'objet n'est pas forcément mauvais.

bargain = a thing bought for less than its usual price. “A real bargain!” = a very good price.

A2: “I bought a book for £5 — a real bargain!” The price is low, and the book is good. A bargain is a good price, not a cheap thing.

Try it: sale / bargain — the sale is the event, the bargain is the thing you buy. “The sale is on. I found a bargain.”

WORDS　B1　more / less

Explication | comparative, le comparatif : more than / less than pour parler de quantité et de taille.

more than = a bigger number than. less than = a smaller number than. £100 is more than £5. £5 is less than £100.

B1: “£100 is more than the £5 I spent.” With numbers, use more than and less than: more people, less money, more seats, less time.

Try it: “The film is longer than the trailer.” “The book is cheaper than the film.”

GRAMMAR　A2　will + verb

Explication | will + infinitif : pour ce qui va se passer ; dans les questions, will se met au début.

The sales will end on November 30. Advent will begin on Sunday. Will the price fall again? Nobody knows.

Rule: will + verb, no -s, no -ed. “She will buy.” “They will watch.” Question: “Will they shop on Friday?”

GRAMMAR　B1　have done

Explication | present perfect : have/has + participe passé — pour ce qui a commencé et vaut encore.

The sales have started already. The shops have opened early. “Have started” = they started, and they are still on.

Rule: have/has + past participle. “I have bought a book.” “She has lit the candle.” Question: “Have you bought a bargain?”

GRAMMAR　B2　headlines

Explication | headline present = le présent des titres : les journaux racontent au présent ce qui s'est déjà passé.

Newspapers often use the present in headlines for what already happened: “Shops Open Early for Black Friday” — the shops opened, but the headline says Open. The present makes the news feel fresh. This issue's headline does the opposite on purpose: “Black Friday Week: The Sales Started Early” uses Started, the past — because the news is the early start, not the day itself.

GRAMMAR　B2　if + will

Explication | first conditional, la première forme du conditionnel : if + présent, will + verbe — pour ce qui peut arriver.

If you buy early, you will save money. If the price falls, I will buy two. If = when this happens, that happens.
